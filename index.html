The Invisible Threat

The Global Tire Microplastic Crisis

While global policy targets single-use plastics and tailpipe emissions, vehicular tire friction remains an unaddressed titan in aquatic micro-pollution.

6+ Million Tons Annually

Vehicular transport generates massive synthetic rubber debris that sheds off tread blocks directly into terrestrial soil, urban air, and storm drains worldwide.

28% Ocean Microplastics

Tire wear particles represent the single largest identifiable source of primary microplastics in aquatic environments, surpassing synthetic textile washing.

EV Weight & Torque Acceleration

Heavy Electric Vehicles (EVs) are 20-30% heavier due to battery packs and deliver instant torque, accelerating tread degradation rates by up to 30%.

Chemical Threat Highlight: 6PPD-Quinone

Standard tires rely on 6PPD chemical preservatives which transform into toxic 6PPD-quinone upon atmospheric oxidation. When rain washes wear debris into river systems, 6PPD-q causes acute mortality in salmonids and aquatic ecosystems.

Eco-Toxicity Risk: High

Chemical Engineering

Interactive Nano-Reinforcement Breakdown

Discover how reinforcing raw synthetic tire compound with graphene sheets and Dicumyl Peroxide (DCP) covalent cross-linking locks polymer chains together.

STEP 01 // MECHANICAL BARRIER

Graphene Sheet Barrier Matrix

Monolayer to few-layer graphene sheets are dispersed into the standard synthetic rubber polymer compound. Due to graphene's extraordinary tensile strength (>130 GPa) and thermal conductivity, it acts as a physical shear barrier.

  • Restricts polymer movement under heavy cornering/braking shear stress.
  • Suppresses micro-crack propagation before particles tear off.
  • Dissipates frictional heat evenly across the tread surface.
Hexagonal Carbon Nanostructure

Physical barrier preventing elastomer chain slippage under load.

STEP 02 // COVALENT BONDING

Dicumyl Peroxide (DCP) Cross-Linking

Standard graphene often suffers from agglomeration (clumping) inside raw rubber. Dicumyl Peroxide (DCP) acts as an organic peroxide curing agent that thermal-decomposes into active peroxide radicals.

  • Radicals abstract hydrogen from synthetic rubber backbones & graphene functional sites.
  • Creates direct, ultra-strong Carbon-Carbon (C-C) covalent bonds.
  • Forms a unified 3D stress-distribution matrix throughout the elastomer.
3D C-C Covalent Network

Eliminates graphene clumping and Locks rubber chains under high torque.

STEP 03 // FACTORY COMPATIBILITY

Drop-In Vulcanization Integration

The DCP-Graphene additive system fits seamlessly into existing industrial rubber compounding and thermal vulcanization machinery. No factory re-tooling is required.

  • Integrates into Banbury mixers during initial masterbatch compounding.
  • Activates fully within standard industrial curing temperature profiles.
  • Zero alteration required for standard mold geometry or bead construction.
100% Drop-in Factory Compatible
